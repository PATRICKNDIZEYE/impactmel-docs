# A programme's page

Every programme has its own page. It answers the question a director asks — *how is this programme doing?* — without opening each project in turn and adding up.

To open it, go to **Programmes** in the top bar and click the programme's **name**. Clicking anywhere else on the row just expands it to show the projects underneath.

{{figure:programme-page}}

---

## The header

At the top you get the programme's code and name, its start and end dates, and its description.

Two buttons sit on the right:

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Tracking table** | Opens the organisation-wide tracking table, filtered to this programme |
| **Edit** | Opens the programme's edit form |

**← All programmes** takes you back to the portfolio.

---

## How the programme is doing

A row of six figures sits under the header:

- **Projects** — how many projects are in the programme
- **Indicators** — how many indicators those projects hold between them
- **On track**, **At risk**, **Off track** and **No data** — how those indicators are performing

The four status figures are links. Click one and you land in the indicator register, filtered to this programme and that status. That is the fastest route from "seven are off track" to "which seven".

::: tip The bands are set by your organisation
On track, at risk and off track are worked out from the thresholds in **Settings → Organization**, under **When progress is on track**. Change them there and every status in the product follows.
:::

---

## Questions waiting to be confirmed

If any of the programme's indicators were set up from your own documents, a band appears saying how many points are still waiting for your team to confirm, with a link reading **Go through them**.

These are things that were read from a document rather than stated outright. Check them before anyone reports against those indicators.

---

## The projects, with their indicators

The rest of the page is one block per project.

Each block's header shows the project code and name — click it to open the project — its dates, how many indicators it holds, and a **Performance** link that opens the project's Performance Review.

Underneath, up to six of the project's indicators are listed. Each line shows:

- The indicator code and name, linked to the indicator
- Its level, as a small badge
- A note reading **also in 2 other** where the same definition is used elsewhere
- What was reported against the whole-plan target — *1,240 of 2,000*
- Its status, as a coloured pill with a written label

Where a project has more than six indicators, the last line reads **All 14 indicators in this project** and opens the indicator register filtered to it.

A project with no indicators shows only its header.

---

## When a programme has no projects

The page says **No projects in this programme yet**, and tells you that adding one will roll its indicators up here.

To add one: go back to **Programmes** and use **Add Project** on the programme's row. That pre-selects the programme on the new project form.

## What a reporter sees here

You see the programme, but only the projects you are assigned to. If you are assigned to none, the page will look empty. That is your assignment, not a fault.

The **Edit** button will not work for you.
